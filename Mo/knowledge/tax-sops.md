Standard Procedure: Business Entity Profit &
Loss Requirement for Tax Preparation
Purpose
To ensure all business income is accurately reported and eliminate the risk of omitted
income, every business entity included in a tax engagement must have supporting
financial statements attached to the Monday board before the return is prepared or
reviewed.


Background
During the preparation of multiple client’s tax returns, it was discovered that Forms 1099
had been issued under both her individual name and her corporation. Because a Profit &
Loss Statement was not prepared for the corporation, the corporate income reported on
the 1099s was not included in the business return, resulting in omitted income that now
requires an amendment.

This situation highlighted the need for a standardized review process to verify that all
business activity has been identified, reconciled, and included before filing.


Standard Procedure
For every client with any of the following business entities:

   ●​ Sole Proprietorship (Schedule C)
   ●​ Single-Member LLC
   ●​ Multi-Member LLC
   ●​ Partnership
   ●​ S Corporation
   ●​ C Corporation

A Profit & Loss Statement is required and must be attached to the client's Monday
board before the tax return is considered ready for review.

The Profit & Loss Statement should be prepared using one or more of the
following:
   ●​ QuickBooks Online or other accounting software
   ●​ Forms 1099 received
   ●​ Bank or transaction activity
   ●​ Bookkeeping records
   ●​ Income and expense spreadsheets provided by the client

If the business had no activity during the tax year, a Profit & Loss Statement must still
be attached showing $0 income and $0 expenses. This documents that the business
activity was reviewed and confirms that a zero-activity return is being filed.


Balance Sheet Requirement
If the business reports $250,000 or more in annual revenue, a Balance Sheet must also
be attached to the Monday board before preparation and review are completed.


Verification Requirements
Before the tax return is submitted, verify that:

   ●​ A Profit & Loss Statement is attached for every business entity.
   ●​ A Balance Sheet is attached when required.
   ●​ Total income reported on the Profit & Loss Statement reconciles to all Forms
      1099, bookkeeping records, QuickBooks reports, and other supporting financial
      documents.
   ●​ Any differences between financial statements and tax documents have been
      investigated and documented.
   ●​ Zero-activity businesses include supporting documentation confirming no
      business activity occurred during the tax year.


Objective
This procedure creates a standardized verification process that ensures every business
entity is supported by financial statements, all business income has been reconciled
before filing, and omissions caused by missing financial reports are prevented. It also
provides reviewers with a complete set of supporting documentation, improving the
accuracy, consistency, and quality of every business tax return.
